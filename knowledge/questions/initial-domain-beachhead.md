# Initial Domain Beachhead

## Question

Which specific quantitative domain should serve as the initial beachhead to validate Connected Models, attract early paying users or agent workloads, and prove that connected graphs outperform siloed models?

## Why it matters

The vision of Connected Models is universal ("all the world's models in one place"). However, without an initial vertical beachhead, the project risks spreading too thin across divergent domains (e.g., trying to solve climate change, pharmacology, and portfolio management all at once). To prove viability and attract the first paying clients or active agent deployments, the project must demonstrate overwhelming utility on a specific, high-value problem class.

## Current partial answer

Legacy notes explore several candidate verticals:
- **Corporate Valuation / Intrinsic Value**: Highly formalized, clear commercial value, and already prototyped with the DuPont ROI and Intrinsic Value Mermaid graphs ([[idea-domain-exemplars-intrinsic-value-and-fx]]).
- **Foreign Exchange / Macro Forecasting**: Concrete models (CIP, UIP, PPP, ARIMA) with daily empirical market truth to evaluate predictive accuracy.
- **Prediction Markets / Autonomous Trading**: Direct economic payoff for bias-free quantitative estimates.
- **Climate & Physical Risk**: Immense societal value, but data pipelines and model runtimes are extraordinarily complex.

## What would resolve it?

1. Constructing a concrete end-state vision: what does the deliverable look like in the candidate domain?
2. Quantifying the delta: what specifically gets cheaper, faster, or more accurate when models are connected versus isolated?
3. Identifying the first three paying customer profiles or target agent reasoning workflows.
