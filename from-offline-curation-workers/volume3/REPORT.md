## Deferred

- Lower-of-cost-and-net-realisable-value inventory measurement: Financial Statement Analysis Learning Module 5, printed p278/PDF p292: carrying value is min(cost, NRV). Full general relationship requires a min operator or piecewise conditional, neither supported in the scalar engine; do not substitute an unguarded branch. Public IAS 2 overview: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-2-inventories/
- IFRS recoverable amount and impairment loss: Learning Module 6 printed pp362–364/PDF pp376–378: recoverable amount is max(fair value less disposal costs, value in use); loss applies only when carrying amount exceeds recoverable amount. Full relationship requires max and conditional operators unsupported by the engine. Public IAS 36 overview: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-36-impairment-of-assets/
- IFRS impairment reversal cap: Learning Module 6 printed p365/PDF p379: reversal is limited by the carrying amount that would have existed absent impairment; general calculation requires a minimum and conditional operator. Public IAS 36 overview: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-36-impairment-of-assets/
- General FIFO and LIFO inventory-layer allocation: Financial Statement Analysis LM5, printed pp259–265/PDF pp273–279, Examples 2–3: complete allocation across an arbitrary number of inventory layers requires priority ordering plus conditional depletion or min/max. The scalar/family engine does not provide the necessary elementwise conditional allocation. Do not replace the general algorithm with an unguarded arithmetic branch; periodic weighted-average allocation is covered separately in Volume 1.
- IRR and yield-to-maturity root solving: Corporate Issuers LM4, printed pp739–741/PDF pp753–755, equations 3–4; Financial Statement Analysis LM8 printed pp448–449/PDF pp462–463: finding IRR or yield from price and a nontrivial stream generally requires numerical root finding, which is outside the engine. Nonconventional cash flows can have multiple IRRs.
- Uneven raw-cash-flow net present value in a single expression: Corporate Issuers LM4 printed pp737–739/PDF pp751–753, equations 1–2: the full arbitrary-time raw-cash-flow formula requires elementwise discounting of indexed cash flows before summation, which the present family grammar cannot express directly. Existing Volume 1 cash-flow additivity supports a precomputed same-date value family and the supplied lump-sum model discounts each element externally.
- General residual-floor-capped declining-balance depreciation: Financial Statement Analysis LM9, printed pp540–542 (PDF pp554–556), Exhibits 22–23 and Exhibit 23 footnote 2: selecting a normal declining-balance charge or a residual-limited charge requires min/conditional dispatch; the runtime supports neither. The ordinary uncapped branch and final-period adjustment are returned separately, without claiming they automatically implement the complete policy.

## Source discrepancies

- Financial Statement Analysis LM7, printed p426 (PDF p440), Example 5: Example states the asset deferred-tax liability is 56,000 after excluding the 120,000 effect of revaluation from 176,000. Under the stated 40% tax rate and 440,000 taxable temporary difference, this appears to conflate the location of recognition outside profit or loss with the total deferred-tax liability. IAS 12 (2021), paragraphs 61A–62 and Example B, recognizes the revaluation-related deferred tax in other comprehensive income; verify exceptions and assumptions before treating the example as an oracle. Corroboration: https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-112-mar-2020
- Corporate Issuers LM3, printed pp699–700 (PDF pp713–714), restaurant example: The restaurant example states fixed costs of EUR250,000, but its operating-profit arithmetic subsequently subtracts EUR50,000 while reporting EUR250,000 profit from EUR1,000,000 revenue and EUR500,000 variable costs. The reported profit and 25% margin are consistent with EUR250,000 fixed costs; EUR50,000 is inconsistent. Review the source before using that particular intermediate figure. Corroboration: https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/3-1-explain-contribution-margin-and-calculate-contribution-margin-per-unit-contribution-margin-ratio-and-total-contribution-margin
- Financial Statement Analysis LM8, printed p473 (PDF p487), Example 12 cash-flow table: Year 1 financing outflow is printed as 62,902, but Example 11 shows principal repayment 62,092; only 62,092 plus interest of 37,908 equals the stated 100,000 payment. Review the printed figure before using it. Corroboration: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-16-leases/
- Financial Statement Analysis LM8, printed p471 (PDF p485), lessee operating leases under US GAAP: The text states that the ROU asset and lease liability always equal each other for an operating lease. This holds for the simplified example but is not general: FASB explains adjustments for prepaid or accrued payments, incentives, initial direct costs and impairment. Corroboration: https://storage.fasb.org/ASU_2016-02_Section_C.pdf
- Financial Statement Analysis LM8, printed p454 (PDF p468), Example 4 solution 4: The source divides a 44,518 premium by five and reports 8,903.64, with interest expense 41,096.36. The stated inputs instead give amortization 8,903.60 and expense 41,096.40. The curated record uses the arithmetic implied by the stated inputs. Corroboration: https://openstax.org/books/principles-financial-accounting/pages/13-2-compute-amortization-of-long-term-liabilities-using-the-effective-interest-method
- Corporate Issuers LM5, printed pp783–784 (PDF pp797–798), Example 9 option E: The example seeks 200,000 net cash but describes selling 200,000 of securities with a 0.5% brokerage cost, which yields only 199,000 net if the fee is deducted from proceeds. Corroboration: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1049442/000110465906026537/a06-8764_3ex99d2.htm

The solution twice labels its numerator TWD237.275 million while reporting USD7.67 billion at TWD30.95/USD. Corroboration: https://news.acer.com/acer-reports-fiscal-2017-results
